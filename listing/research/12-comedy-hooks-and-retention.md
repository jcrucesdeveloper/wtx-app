Researched 2026-10-06 after batch C was judged "better, but the hook has to
improve in the first seconds". Batch C fixed the *tone* (11); this is about
the **first two seconds and the retention curve of a joke**, which is a
different problem from hooking an explainer (10): a joke cannot open on its
punchline. About 21 pages read. Vendor numbers are direction only.

## What the evidence says

| # | Finding | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The first second is the thumbnail.** The choice is made in ~0.5–1 s, before the text is read: "viewed vs swiped away" on Shorts, the 2-second view on TikTok. The first frame must work **as a still and on mute**. Target: 70 %+ still there at 2 s. | Platform metrics + vendors |
| 2 | **Read the curve.** A *cliff* at 1–2 s = the first frame failed. A *slope* from 3–8 s = the hook worked but the payoff is slow: "deliver the first value by second 5", add a **second hook at 3–5 s**, change something every 2–3 s. A *drop at the end* = the ending lingers: **end abruptly, right after the payoff**. A *bump* = people re-watched that moment: move it earlier next time. | Vendor, but it is how the graph works |
| 3 | **For comedy: "jump straight into the punchline or a scenario that leaves them wanting more"**; the first reaction beat inside 1–3 s; cut everything before the first interesting thing. | Vendors |
| 4 | **In medias res**: start in the middle of the crisis. It works because the viewer has to work out how we got here. But **anchor it**: who, where, one unknown. Too many unknowns just confuses. (The cold open exists for the same reason: to stop a channel change.) | Writing craft + Wikipedia |
| 5 | **A visual hook is a pattern break**: motion in frame 0, a snap zoom at 0.2–0.5 s, an extreme close-up or odd angle, one impossible thing, an expressive face. The *von Restorff* effect: the item that violates the pattern is the one noticed and remembered. | Vendors + academic |
| 6 | **Sound from frame one**: lead with the catchiest/loudest moment, never a build; audio energy must match the picture. Comedy: over-the-top effects (boing, thud, record scratch). | Vendors |
| 7 | **Text hook**: top of the frame, on screen inside 0.5 s, readable for 2–4 s, bold with an outline or box. 80–85 % start muted. Formats that fit a joke: *POV:*, *When you…*, *The one who…*. | Vendors |
| 8 | **Rewatches are worth the most** (one vendor's weights: rewatch 10, completion 5, like 1; a loop took a video from 85 % to 115 % retention and 4× the impressions). Loops work under ~15 s; end at the payoff; the last beat should make the first second mean more; **do not let the audio give the loop away**; hide a detail worth a second look. | Vendor |
| 9 | **Peak–end rule**: an experience is judged by its most intense moment and its last one. End on the biggest laugh. | Academic |
| 10 | **Comedy length**: Shorts data says 18–28 s for comedy; under 15 s loops better. Ours run 6–10 s, which is right for loops if every second earns its place. Cuts every 2–4 s, but "faster cuts can make a video feel nervous instead of alive". | Vendor |
| 11 | **Misdirection**: an audience attends to one thing at a time; point them at one thing so the turn comes from somewhere else. | Wikipedia |

The pattern: **frame 0 is a calm, normal scene, and the characters are small
on a beige page.** As a still it gives no reason to stop. The first strange

slope. Several also linger after the punchline.

## Rules for batch D

1. **Frame 0 is already wrong.** Something impossible or absurd is visible in
   the still (a T-rex in a gym, a man flying off a treadmill, a face filling
   the frame mid-sneeze). It must not be the final twist: it is the *situation*.
2. **Big and bold.** Characters fill the frame (often a close-up that snaps
   out to reveal the scene); a saturated flat colour per reel, not beige, so
   each one breaks the feed's pattern.
3. **A loud sound and a snap of motion at t = 0.**
4. **First laugh by 1.5 s.** Something new every ~1.5 s. No "normal" beats.
5. **A second hook at 40–50 %**: a second caption or a turn that re-opens the question.
6. **End on the peak and cut.** Nothing after the biggest laugh; the last
   frame hands back to the first (loop), and the audio does not announce it.
7. **The caption is still a meme caption**, top of frame, ≤ 8 words, on frame 0;
   it anchors who/where so the cold open is not confusing.
8. Everything from batch C stays: one joke, no branding, crude drawing,
   faces act, gibberish voices, the plate hides in every scene.
