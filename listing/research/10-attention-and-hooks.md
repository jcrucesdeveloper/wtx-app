## What the evidence says

| # | Finding | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The decision is made in about 1–1.5 s** (some sources say 0.9 s). TikTok counts a "hook" as a **2-second view ÷ impressions**; baseline 30–35 %, elite 40 %+ (Meta: 3 s, 25–30 %, elite 35–45 %). Above **65 % still watching at 3 s** earns "4–7× more impressions". | Vendors; the metric definitions are standard |
| 2 | **Outcome / payoff first beats story set-up.** Proof-first and "result in frame one" hooks remove "payoff doubt"; OpusClip's 13.5 M-clip study found story-setup hooks the worst performers (already in 08). | Large dataset, one vendor |
| 3 | **Negative framing wins**: "stop doing X", unpopular opinion, diagnosis. 1.3–1.8× the hook rate of the same idea stated positively; specific numbers beat round ones ("72.5", not "70-ish"). | Vendor, consistent across 4 pages |
| 4 | **A hook has three layers: visual, text, audio.** Text 3–7 words, on screen within **0.5 s**, readable 2–3 s, high contrast. The **mute test**: silence the first 3 s: if the picture alone doesn't hook, it's weak. | 5 pages agree |
| 5 | **A pattern interrupt must connect to the content**: a sudden zoom, hard cut, odd prop, loud sound, black frame, mid-action start. Random chaos doesn't hold. A surprising first frame is linked to "60 %+ higher retention" (vendor). | Vendors |
| 6 | **Start mid-action**, already engaged ("mid-sentence drop", "interrupted setup"), instead of an idle wind-up. | 3 pages |
| 7 | **Sound in frame 1**: recognisable audio in the first 3 s → "40 % more likely to stay past 5 s" (vendor). A hit, not a build. Trending sound is "a multiplier, not a strategy". | Weak numbers |
| 8 | **Eyes and faces pull attention, and where they look steers it** (eye tracking: a face looking at the product makes viewers look there). Big eyes, large head, round forms are the "baby schema" that cartoonists exaggerate on purpose. | Eye-tracking write-up + Wikipedia |
| 9 | **Movement and sudden change trigger the orienting response**; emotional content habituates slowly, neutral content fast. Guided motion (a pointing arrow, a look) beats chaotic motion. | Wikipedia + PPC Hero |
| 10 | **Curiosity is strongest on a prediction mismatch with a closable gap** (Berlyne; Loewenstein). The Zeigarnik "unfinished = remembered" memory effect failed replication, but the urge to **resume** an interrupted task is real (Ovsiankina). So: open a gap you can see closing, don't just withhold. | Academic |
| 11 | **After the hook, retention is "progressive novel information", not cut speed** (MrBeast's retention strategist): one new thing every 3–5 s (up to 10–15 s for narrative), micro-payoffs at 5–20 s, main payoff ~20–30 s, a loop or sting in the last 1–2 s. | Interview + vendors |
| 12 | **Loops pay**: loop hooks +80–150 % total views (vendor), best under 20 s. Lo-fi, scrappy beats glossy ("the more it looks like a commercial, the faster a user swipes"). | Vendor |
| 13 | **Test structure, not words**; daily cadence beats sporadic perfection; first 10× video typically post 15–40. | Vendor |

## The Hook Gate: an 8-point check for every reel

1. **Mute test**: the first 3 s, with no text, still make a stranger curious.
2. **Payoff or peak action visible in 0–1 s** (the crush, the KO, the record), not the set-up.
3. **Motion in frame 0**: something is moving or changing within 0.3 s.
4. **Text hook**: 3–7 words, on screen at 0, high contrast, a specific number or a negative/diagnosis frame.
5. **A sound event at t = 0** (a hit), not a quiet bed that builds.
6. **Eyes or a "wait, what?" image** in frame 0; the character looks at the viewer.
7. **A new thing every 2–4 s**, with a micro-payoff by ~5 s.
8. **The ending links back to frame 0** (loop) or lands a 1–2 s sting.

The pattern: **every reel builds to its payoff, which is exactly the story-set-up
shape the data ranks worst.** Eleven of twelve are silent or quiet at t = 0.
We optimised for a satisfying 14 s watch; the feed is decided in the first 1.5 s.

## What we change (the "b" series)

Each reel gets a **cold open** (1.2–1.8 s, a whole number of beats) in front of
the story, built from the Hook Gate:

- **The payoff first**: the crushed stick figure, the shouting Placa, the K.O.,
  the loaded bar, the record card, the thrown-away notebook … in frame 0.
- **A 3–7 word text hook, negative or numeric** ("¿75? ¿60? Ni idea.",
  "Ella sabe que no entrenaste", "Deja de anotar en Notas").
- **A sound hit at t = 0** and a motion/zoom punch in the first frames.
- **A rewind** (tape scrub, sound and glitch) back to the story, a visible
  "how did we get here?" gap that the next seconds close.
- The original reel stays as the control, so we can compare *same body, new
  hook* (research: test structure, not wording).

Shared code: `A.shift` (runs an existing composition later on the timeline
behind a cover), `A.rewind`, the `rewind` sound, and `compositions/src/cold-opens.js`.
